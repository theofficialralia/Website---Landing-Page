How Can I Create an Effective Digital Marketing Strategy for My Business?

Most businesses don’t have a marketing problem.

They have a trust problem.

You can post every day.
Run Facebook and Instagram ads.
Send beautiful emails.
Design professional graphics.
Track every click.

And still hear:

“Let me think about it.”

Why?

Because your customers already know that you want to sell to them.

The smarter question is:

Who do they trust enough to listen to?

That is where a modern digital marketing strategy changes.

Nielsen research has repeatedly found that recommendations from people consumers know rank among the most trusted forms of advertising. Its studies have reported figures ranging from 88% in 2021 to 92% in an earlier global study, well ahead of many conventional digital advertising formats.

So the winning strategy isn’t social media OR email OR advertising OR word of mouth.

It’s:

Build your digital infrastructure then put trust on top of it.

And that is exactly where platforms such as Ralia can fit into the marketing stack.

The 5 Part Digital Marketing System

A strong strategy can be reduced to five questions:

  1. Who are you trying to reach?
  2. Where do they spend attention?
  3. What will make them trust you?
  4. What action do you want them to take?
  5. How will you know it worked?

Everything else supports these five decisions.

1. Start With the Customer Not the Platform

Don’t begin with:

“Should we advertise on Instagram?”

Begin with:

“Who is most likely to buy, and what would make them believe us?”

Build a simple customer profile:

  • Who: age, location, occupation or business type
  • Problem: what are they struggling with?
  • Desire: what outcome do they actually want?
  • Objection: why might they hesitate?
  • Trigger: what would make them act now?
  • Trust source: whose opinion would influence them?

That last question is becoming increasingly important.

A customer may ignore your company’s post.

But they may stop scrolling when someone they recognise or someone who appears genuinely relevant talks about your product.

2. Build Your Social Media Strategy Around Attention → Trust → Action

Posting consistently is not a strategy.

A content system is.

Use this simple framework:

A. Attention

Create content that earns the first three seconds.

Examples:

  • “Your customers aren’t ignoring your business. They may simply not trust your marketing.”
  • “3 reasons Nigerian businesses spend money on ads and still struggle to convert.”
  • “If you’re getting views but no sales, check this first.”

B. Education

Show customers that you understand their problem.

Use:

  • How to content
  • Mistakes
  • Comparisons
  • Industry insights
  • Demonstrations
  • FAQs
  • Case studies

C. Proof

This is where many businesses are weak.

Don’t simply say: “We’re the best.”

Show:

  • Customer experiences
  • Testimonials
  • Results
  • Reviews
  • User generated content
  • Before and after outcomes
  • Real people using the product

D. Action

Give the customer one obvious next step.

Buy. Book. Register. Message us. Visit the website.

Don’t make people figure out what to do next.

The Missing Layer: Don’t Make Your Brand Do All the Talking

Your company’s social media should explain the value.

But other people can make that value believable.

This is the distinction between:

Brand communication → “Our product is great.”

and

Peer recommendation → “I tried this, and here’s what happened.”

That second form of communication is powerful because it feels less like advertising and more like social proof.

This is why word of mouth deserves a deliberate place in your digital strategy, not merely something you hope happens naturally.

A platform such as Ralia is designed around this layer: connecting businesses and campaigns with real promoters and creators who can share, recommend and engage with offers and content.

Think of it as the layer between:

“We created the campaign.” and “People actually believe it.”

5. Build an Email Funnel That Keeps Customers

Getting the sale is not the end of marketing.

It’s the beginning of retention.

A simple email sequence can look like this:

Email 1: Welcome
“You’re officially part of the community.” Set expectations and establish the relationship.

Email 2: Value
Give them something genuinely useful. Don’t immediately ask for another purchase.

Email 3: Proof
Show how other customers are getting value.

Email 4: Offer
Present the relevant product, service or promotion.

Email 5: Follow up
Address the hesitation: “Still considering it?” Then provide the information needed to make the decision.

Email 6: Retention
After purchase: “Here’s how to get more from what you bought.”

This is where many businesses stop thinking like marketers and start thinking like relationship builders.

Your Full Customer Journey

That’s a marketing system, not just a collection of posts.

6. Measure What Actually Matters

Likes can feel good. They don’t necessarily pay your bills.

Your KPIs should follow the customer journey.

Reach: Reach equals how many unique people saw your campaign. Useful for measuring awareness.

Engagement: Track comments, shares, saves, clicks, video completion, and engagement rate. Shares and meaningful interactions show that people found something worth passing on.

Conversion Rate: The metric that connects attention to action.
Conversion Rate = Conversions ÷ Relevant Visitors/Clicks × 100
For example, if 1,000 people visit your landing page and 50 purchase, your conversion rate is 5%.

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):
CAC = Total acquisition spend ÷ Number of new customers
If you spend ₦500,000 acquiring 100 customers, CAC = ₦5,000. Now compare that against the value those customers generate.

Retention: Measure repeat purchase rate, customer retention rate, churn, repeat revenue, and customer lifetime value. Acquiring someone once is useful. Getting them to come back is a business.

7. Add Trust to Your KPI Dashboard

Here’s the KPI most marketing teams overlook: How much of our growth is coming from people talking about us?

Track:

  • Promoter generated reach
  • Referral traffic
  • Shares
  • UGC created
  • Creator and promoter engagement
  • Referral conversions
  • Cost per acquisition from promoters
  • Revenue generated through advocacy

This gives you something traditional advertising dashboards often don’t show clearly: whether customers are merely seeing your message or helping spread it.

The Digital Marketing Strategy I’d Build Today

For a Nigerian business starting or rebuilding its digital marketing engine:

  1. Define one high value customer segment.
  2. Identify their biggest problem and buying trigger.
  3. Create content around attention → education → proof → action.
  4. Use Meta, Google, TikTok, WhatsApp or other relevant channels to reach them.
  5. Build a landing page that converts attention into an identifiable lead or customer.
  6. Use email or WhatsApp to nurture and retain customers.
  7. Activate promoters and creators to generate peer to peer recommendations, UGC and social proof.
  8. Track reach → engagement → conversion → CAC → retention → advocacy.
  9. Double down on what produces customers, not merely what produces impressions.

The Real Question

The internet has made it incredibly easy for every business to become a broadcaster.

Everyone can advertise. Everyone can create a beautiful Instagram page. Everyone can boost a post. Everyone can say: “We’re trusted.”

The competitive advantage increasingly comes from something harder to manufacture: other people being willing to talk about you.

Nielsen’s research has consistently placed personal recommendations among the most trusted forms of advertising, while its more recent work specifically recommends leaning into word of mouth rather than abandoning traditional advertising.

So don’t throw away your ads. Make them work harder.

Don’t abandon email. Use it to retain the customers you already earned.

Don’t stop posting. Make your content worth sharing.

And don’t leave word of mouth to chance. Build it into the system.

That is the opportunity Ralia is built around: giving businesses a structured way to activate real people, promoters and creators around campaigns rather than relying entirely on the brand itself to do the talking.

Before your next campaign, ask yourself one question: Are you spending more money making people see your brand, or giving people a reason to recommend it? Because reach gets attention.
